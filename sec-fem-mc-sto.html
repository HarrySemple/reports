    Section 1.6 Stochastic Analysis

    Subsection 1.6.1 Introduction

    Now we consider the case of inexact computation of the scattering form, \(s(\cdot,\cdot)\text{,}\) through Monte-Carlo integration.
    🔗
    That is, we approximate the nonlocal term of (1.2.1) as
    \begin{equation} \int_\Omega \mathcal{K}(x,y)u(y)\mathrm{d}y \approx \frac{|\Omega|}{M} \sum_{j=1}^M \mathcal{K}(x,Y_j)u(Y_j)\tag{1.6.1} \end{equation}
    where \(\{y_j\}_{j=1}^M\) are random samples from \(\Omega\text{.}\)
    🔗
    This leads us to a modified iterative finite element problem to be solved in practice. Given an initial estimate \(u_h^{(0)}\in V_h\text{,}\) we compute for all \(n\in\mathbb{N}\)
    \begin{equation} a(u_h^{(n)}, v) = s_M(u_h^{(n-1)}, v) + l(v) \qquad \forall v\in V_h,\tag{1.6.2} \end{equation}
    where
    \begin{equation} s_M(u,v) := \int_{\Omega} (\mathcal{S}_M u)\, v \, \mathrm{d}x + \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K (\mathcal{S}_M u)\,(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x ,\tag{1.6.3} \end{equation}
    where
    \begin{equation} (\mathcal{S}_M u)(x) := \sigma_T(x) \frac{|\Omega|}{M} \sum_{j=1}^M \mathcal{K}(x,Y_j)u(Y_j),\tag{1.6.4} \end{equation}
    for random draws \(\{y_j\}_{j=1}^M\) from \(\Omega\text{.}\)
    🔗
    To account for this in our analysis, we similarly modify the theoretical steady-state finite element problem. Placing \(s_M\) in (1.4.8), we seek to find \(u_h\in V_h\) such that
    \begin{equation} a(u_h, v) = s_M(u_h, v) + l(v) \qquad \forall v\in V_h.\tag{1.6.5} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.6.2 FE Steady State Error

    We must modify the analysis for our previous Best Approximation result (Theorem 1.5.9) in light of \(u\) and \(u_h\) satisfying different variational formulations. Indeed, the true solution \(u\) still solves the variational form (1.4.8) while \(u_h\) is now treated as the solution to (1.6.5) as the steady state of \(\{u_h^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_0}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Theorem 1.6.1. Strang-Type Error Estimate.

    Let \(u\in H^1_0(\Omega)\) be the solution of (1.2.1) and \(u_h\) the solution of (1.6.5). Then,
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u - u_h} \leq (1 + C_a)\, \inf_{w_h \in V_h} \snorm{u - w_h} + \sup_{v_h \in V_h} \frac{\left| s(u, v_h) - s_M(u_h, v_h) \right|}{\enorm{v_h}}.\tag{1.6.6} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Let \(w_h\in V_h\) be arbitrary. We define \(e_h = w_h - u_h\in V_h\text{.}\) By Lemma 1.5.1 and by definition of \(u_h\text{,}\) we have that
    \begin{equation} a(u-u_h,e_h) = s(u,e_h) - s_M(u_h,e_h).\tag{1.6.7} \end{equation}
    Splitting the first argument of \(a(\cdot,\cdot)\) with \(w_h\text{,}\) we have:
    \begin{equation} a(w_h-u_h,e_h) = a(w_h-u,e_h) + s(u,e_h) - s_M(u_h,e_h).\tag{1.6.8} \end{equation}
    By coercivity of \(a(\cdot,\cdot)\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{align} \frac{1}{2}\enorm{e_h}^2 &\leq a(e_h,e_h)\tag{1.6.9}\\ &= a(w_h - u_h,e_h)\tag{1.6.10}\\ &= a(w_h-u,e_h) + s(u,e_h) - s_M(u_h,e_h)\tag{1.6.11}\\ &\leq C_a \snorm{w_h-u}\enorm{e_h} + s(u,e_h) - s_M(u_h,e_h).\tag{1.6.12} \end{align}
    Where the last inequality is due to continuity of \(a(\cdot,\cdot)\text{.}\) Now, we have
    \begin{align} s(u,e_h) - s_M(u_h,e_h) &= \enorm{e_h}\frac{s(u,e_h) - s_M(u_h,e_h)}{\enorm{e_h}}\tag{1.6.13}\\ &\leq \enorm{e_h}\sup_{v_h\in V_h}\frac{|s(u,v_h) - s_M(u_h,v_h)|}{\enorm{v_h}}.\tag{1.6.14} \end{align}
    Thus, we have
    \begin{equation} \enorm{w_h - u_h} \leq C_a \snorm{w_h-u} + \sup_{v_h\in V_h}\frac{|s(u,v_h) - s_M(u_h,v_h)|}{\enorm{v_h}}.\tag{1.6.15} \end{equation}
    The result follows from the triangle inequality,
    \begin{align} \enorm{u-u_h} &\leq \enorm{u - w_h} + \enorm{w_h - u_h}\tag{1.6.16}\\ &\leq (1+C_a) \snorm{u-w_h} + \sup_{v_h\in V_h}\frac{|s(u,v_h) - s_M(u_h,v_h)|}{\enorm{v_h}}.\tag{1.6.17} \end{align}
    and taking the infimum over \(w_h\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 1.6.2. Probabilistic Bound for Monte-Carlo Consistency.

    Suppose that \(\mathcal{K}\in L^\infty(\Omega\times\Omega)\text{.}\) We have that, with probability \(1-\eta\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation} \frac{|s(u,v_h) - s_M(u_h,v_h)|}{\enorm{v_h}} \leq C_s\enorm{u-u_h} + B\sqrt{\frac{2}{M}\log\left(\frac{2}{\eta}\right)}\tag{1.6.18} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    We first split into compatible arguments:
    \begin{align} s(u,v_h) - s_M(u_h,v_h) &= s(u - u_h,v_h) + s(u_h,v_h) - s_M(u_h,v_h)\tag{1.6.19}\\ &\leq C_s\enorm{u-u_h}\enorm{v_h} + \left[s(u_h,v_h) - s_M(u_h,v_h)\right].\tag{1.6.20} \end{align}
    The remainder of this proof focuses on the second term. We have
    \begin{align} s(u_h,v_h) - s_M(u_h,v_h) &= \int_\Omega \left[ \left(\mathcal{S}u_h\right)(x) - \left(\mathcal{S}_M u_h\right)(x) \right]v_h(x) \mathrm{d}x\tag{1.6.21}\\ &\quad + \sum_{K} \int_{K} \delta_K \left[ (\mathcal{S} u_h)(x) - (\mathcal{S}_M u_h)(x) \right]\, (\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v_h(x))\, dx.\tag{1.6.22} \end{align}
    For a test function \(v_h\in V_h\text{,}\) we define the following random variables for \(j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\)
    \begin{align} W_j(v_h) &= |\Omega| \int_{\Omega} \mathcal{K}(x, Y_j)\, u_h(Y_j)\, \sigma_T(x) v_h(x)\, dx\tag{1.6.23}\\ &= |\Omega|\, u_h(Y_j) \int_{\Omega} \mathcal{K}(x, Y_j)\, \sigma_T(x) v_h(x)\, dx,\tag{1.6.24} \end{align}
    \begin{equation} X_j(v_h) = |\Omega|\, u_h(Y_j) \sum_{K} \int_{K} \delta_K \, \sigma_T(x)\, \mathcal{K}(x, Y_j)\, (\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v_h)(x)\, dx.\tag{1.6.25} \end{equation}
    where \(\{Y_j\}_{j=1}^{M}\) are i.i.d uniform random variables on \(\Omega\text{.}\) We have
    \begin{equation} s_M(u_h, v_h) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} (W_j(v_h) + X_j(v_h)).\tag{1.6.26} \end{equation}
    As \(Y_j\) are independent uniform RVs on \(\Omega\text{,}\) this yields an unbiased estimate of the true scattering form:
    \begin{equation} \mathbb{E}[W_j(v_h)+X_j(v_h)] = s(u_h, v_h).\tag{1.6.27} \end{equation}
    In order to apply Hoeffding’s inequality, we must also bound these RVs. The integral inside (1.6.24) is bounded via Cauchy-Schwarz as
    \begin{equation} \left| \int_{\Omega} \mathcal{K}(x, Y_j)\, \sigma_T v_h(x)\, dx \right| \leq \|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^\infty(\Omega\times\Omega)} \linf{\sigma_T} |\Omega|^{1/2} \|v_h\|_{L^2(\Omega)}.\tag{1.6.28} \end{equation}
    Thus, we have
    \begin{equation} \left| W_j(v_h) \right| \leq \underbrace{|\Omega|^{3/2} \, \|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^\infty(\Omega\times\Omega)} \linf{\sigma_T} \, \linf{u_h}\frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}}}_{B_1} \, \enorm{v_h}.\tag{1.6.29} \end{equation}
    Similarly, we have
    \begin{equation} |X_j(v_h)| \leq \underbrace{|\Omega|^{3/2} \, \|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^\infty(\Omega\times\Omega)} \, \linf{u_h} \, \linf{\sigma_T} \, \delta_{\max}^{1/2}}_{B_2} \, \enorm{v_h}.\tag{1.6.30} \end{equation}
    Combining these, we have i.i.d. RVs \(\{Z_j = W_j + X_j\}_{j=1}^M\) bounded by
    \begin{equation} |Z_j|\leq (B_1+B_2)\enorm{v_h} = B\enorm{v_h}.\tag{1.6.31} \end{equation}
    We can thus apply Hoeffding’s inequality,
    \begin{equation} \mathbb{P}\left( \left| \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} Z_j - \mathbb{E}Z_1 \right| \geq t \right) \leq 2 \exp\left( -\frac{M t^2}{2 B^2 \enorm{v_h}^2} \right).\tag{1.6.32} \end{equation}
    Setting \(t = B \enorm{v_h} \sqrt{\frac{2 \log(2/\eta)}{M}}\) gives, with probability at least \(1-\eta\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation} \left| s(u_h, v_h) - s_M(u_h, v_h) \right| \leq B \enorm{v_h} \sqrt{\frac{2 \log(2/\eta)}{M}}.\tag{1.6.33} \end{equation}
    Combining with (1.6.20) and dividing by \(\enorm{v_h}\) yields the result.
    🔗
    🔗

    Corollary 1.6.3. Error Estimate for the Steady State.

    Suppose \(u\in H^1_0(\Omega)\) is the solution of (1.2.1) and \(u_h\) the solution of (1.6.5). Given that \(\mathcal{K}\in L^\infty(\Omega\times\Omega)\) and \(C_s\leq 1\text{,}\) we have with probability at least \(1-\eta\)
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u-u_h} \leq \frac{1+C_a}{1-C_s} C h^{\min\{k+1,r\} - 1/2} |u|_{H^r(\Omega)} + \frac{B}{1-C_s} \sqrt{\frac{2}{M}\log\left(\frac{2}{\eta}\right)}\tag{1.6.34} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    The result follows from combining Theorem 1.6.1 and Proposition 1.6.2. We have, with probability \(1-\eta\)
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u-u_h} \leq (1+C_a)\inf_{w_h\in V_h}\snorm{u-w_h} + C_s \enorm{u - u_h} + B\sqrt{\frac{2}{M}\log\left(\frac{2}{\eta}\right)}.\tag{1.6.35} \end{equation}
    Rearranging for \(\enorm{u-u_h}\) gives the result.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.6.3 FE Source Iteration Error

    The adjustments required for our previous source iteration analysis are comparatively straightforward. Indeed, \(s_M(\cdot,\cdot)\) appears in both (1.6.5) and (1.6.2), meaning we don’t have to use a Monte Carlo consistency bound as for the steady state error analysis.
    🔗
    Instead, all that is needed for our convergence results (Theorem 1.5.12 and Corollary 1.5.13) to carry over is that \(s_M(\cdot,\cdot)\) admits a continuity result equivalent to Lemma 1.5.5. By bounding \(\mathcal{K}\) above by its infinity-norm evaluated everywhere on the domain, we arrive at the following:
    \begin{equation} s_M(w,v) \leq C_s^M \enorm{w}\enorm{v},\tag{1.6.36} \end{equation}
    where
    \begin{equation} C_s^M = \|\sigma_T\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)} \, \|K\|_{L^\infty(\Omega \times \Omega)}|\Omega| \left( 1 + \delta_{\max}^{1/2}\mu^{1/2} \right) \frac{1}{\mu}.\tag{1.6.37} \end{equation}
    This leads directly to an extension of Corollary 1.5.13.
    🔗

    Corollary 1.6.4. Convergence in SUPG-Norm with MC Approximation.

    Let \(u_h\) be the solution of (1.6.5), \(u_h^{(0)}\in V_h\) arbitrary and \(\{u_h^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\) the sequence defined by (1.6.2). Given \(\mathcal{K}\in L^\infty(\Omega\times\Omega)\text{,}\) we have for all \(n\in\mathbb{N}_0\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u_h - u_h^{(n)}} \leq (2C_s^M)^n \enorm{u_h - u_h^{(0)}}.\tag{1.6.38} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    The proof is identical to that of Corollary 1.5.13, using (1.6.36) in place of Lemma 1.5.5.

    Subsection 1.6.4 Combined Error Estimate

    We now combine these strands into a single error estimate.
    🔗

    Theorem 1.6.5. Error Estimate with Monte Carlo Integration.

    Suppose \(u\) is the solution of (1.2.1). Let \(u_h\) be the solution of (1.6.5), \(u_h^{(0)}\in V_h\) arbitrary and \(\{u_h^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\) the sequence defined by (1.6.2). Given \(\mathcal{K}\in L^\infty(\Omega\times\Omega)\text{,}\) we have for all \(n\in\mathbb{N}_0\text{,}\) with probability \(1-\eta\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u - u_h^{(n)}} \leq \tilde{C} h^{\min\{k+1,r\} - 1/2} |u|_{H^r(\Omega)} + \tilde{B} \sqrt{\frac{2}{M}\log\left(\frac{2}{\eta}\right)} + \left( 2 C_s^M \right)^{n} \enorm{u_h - u_h^{(0)}}\tag{1.6.39} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Similar to Theorem 1.5.14, the result follows from splitting \(\enorm{u - u_h^{(n)}}\) with the triangle inequality (1.4.10), then applying Corollary 1.6.3 and Corollary 1.6.4.
