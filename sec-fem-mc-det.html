    Section 1.5 Deterministic Analysis

    Subsection 1.5.1 Introduction

    In this section, we present a preliminary error analysis under the simplifying assumption that \(\mathcal{S}\) is computed exactly. We define the norms used throughout, before establishing well-posedness through verifying the conditions for the Lax-Milgram theorem. We then derive separate bounds for the discretisation error \(\enorm{u-u_h}\) and the iteration error \(\enorm{u_h-u_h^{(n)}}\text{,}\) which combine via the triangle inequality to give the main deterministic estimate. For simplicity, we take \(g=0\) (meaning \(u\in H_0^1\)) from this section onwards - an extension to nonhomogenous inflow data is left to future work.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.5.2 Norms

    We equip \(V_h\) with the SUPG norm, defined as
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:supg-norm} \enorm{v}^2 := \mu\ltwo{v}^2 + \sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v}^2 + \int_{\partial\Omega} |\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}| v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S,\tag{1.5.1} \end{equation}
    where \(\mu\) is the positivity constant defined in(1.2.5) and \(\delta_K\) is the element-wise stabilisation parameter. This immediately results in the following relation of norms, used frequently in later analysis
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:rel-norms} \enorm{v}^2 \geq \mu\ltwo{v}^2 \qquad \forall v\in V_h.\tag{1.5.2} \end{equation}
    For continuity estimates, we also require the stronger star-SUPG norm, given by
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:star-supg-norm} \snorm{v}^2 = \enorm{v}^2 + \sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}} \delta_K^{-1} \ltwok{v}^2.\tag{1.5.3} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.5.3 Well-Posedness and Key Properties

    We now present key properties of the bilinear and linear forms defined in(1.4.5),(1.4.6) and(1.4.7). This will show that the variational problem(1.4.8) is well-posed, and provide the necessary results for error analysis.
    🔗

    Lemma 1.5.1. Consistency of the SUPG Method.

    Let \(u\) be the true solution of(1.4.1). Then for all \(v\in V_h\) we have
    \begin{equation} a(u,v) = s(u,v) + l(v).\tag{1.5.4} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    We have
    \begin{align} a(u,v) - s(u,v) - l(v) & \int_{\Omega} (\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u + \sigma u - \mathcal{S}u - q) v \, \mathrm{d}x\tag{1.5.5}\\ &+ \sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K (\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u + \sigma u - \mathcal{S}u - q)(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v) \, \mathrm{d}x\tag{1.5.6}\\ &- \int_{\Gamma_-} (\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n})(u-g)v\, ds\tag{1.5.7}\\ =& 0.\tag{1.5.8} \end{align}
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 1.5.2. Coercivity of \(a(\cdot,\cdot)\).

    For all \(v\in V_h\) we have
    \begin{equation} a(v,v) \geq \frac{1}{2}\enorm{v}^2.\tag{1.5.9} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    We treat each term in(1.4.5) separately. For the convection term, by Green’s formula(1.3.5) we have
    \begin{align} \int_{\Omega} (\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v)\, v \, \mathrm{d}x &= \frac{1}{2} \int_{\Omega} \mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla (v^2)\, \mathrm{d}x\tag{1.5.10}\\ &= \frac{1}{2} \int_{\partial \Omega} (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n})\, v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S .\tag{1.5.11} \end{align}
    We combine this with the boundary inflow term. Let \(\Gamma_+ = \partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_-\text{.}\) We have
    \begin{align} \frac{1}{2} \int_{\partial \Omega} (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n})\, v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S - \int_{\Gamma_-} (\mathbf{b} &\cdot \mathbf{n})\, v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S \tag{1.5.12}\\ &= \frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_+} (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n})\, v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S - \frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_-} (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n})\, v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S \tag{1.5.13}\\ &= \frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega} |\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n}|\, v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S,\tag{1.5.14} \end{align}
    where the last equality follows from definition of \(\Gamma_-\) in(1.2.2). Combined with the reaction term, we have
    \begin{align} \int_{\Omega} (\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v)\, v + \sigma v^2\, \mathrm{d}x - \int_{\Gamma_-} (\mathbf{b} &\cdot \mathbf{n})\, v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S\tag{1.5.15}\\ &= \int_{\Omega} \sigma v^2\, \mathrm{d}x + \frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega} |\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n}|\, v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S\tag{1.5.16}\\ &\geq \mu\ltwo{v}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega} |\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n}|\, v^2 \, \mathrm{d}S.\tag{1.5.17} \end{align}
    Now, we turn to the SUPG term. We have
    \begin{align} \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v &+ \sigma v)\, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x\tag{1.5.18}\\ &= \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \, |\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v |^2\, \mathrm{d}x + \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \sigma v\, (\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v)\, dx\tag{1.5.19}\\ &= \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \|\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v\|_{L^2(K)}^2 + \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \sigma v\, (\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v)\, dx\tag{1.5.20} \end{align}
    The second term is indefinite, and we bound its contribution with Young’s inequality(1.3.6)
    \begin{equation} \left| \int_K \delta_K \sigma v\, (\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v)\, dx\right| \leq \frac{1}{2}\,\delta_K \|\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v\|_{L^2(K)}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,\delta_K \linf{\sigma}^2 \|v\|_{L^2(K)}^2.\tag{1.5.21} \end{equation}
    Thus, the SUPG Term is bounded below by
    \begin{align} \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v &+ \sigma v)\, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x\tag{1.5.22}\\ &\geq \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \|\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v\|_{L^2(K)}^2 - \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \frac{1}{2}\,\delta_K \|\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v\|_{L^2(K)}^2\tag{1.5.23}\\ &\qquad \qquad - \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \frac{1}{2}\,\delta_K \linf{\sigma}^2 \|v\|_{L^2(K)}^2\tag{1.5.24}\\ &= \frac{1}{2} \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \|\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v\|_{L^2(K)}^2 - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \linf{\sigma}^2 \|v\|_{L^2(K)}^2.\tag{1.5.25} \end{align}
    By(1.4.4), we have \(\delta_K \leq \mu/\linf{\sigma}^2\text{.}\) Thus,
    \begin{align} \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v &+ \sigma v)\, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x\tag{1.5.26}\\ &\geq \frac{1}{2} \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \|\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v\|_{L^2(K)}^2 - \frac{\mu}{2}\ltwo{v}^2\tag{1.5.27} \end{align}
    Adding(1.5.17) and(1.5.27), we have
    \begin{align} a(v, v) &\geq \frac{\mu}{2} \|v\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \sum_K \delta_K \|\mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla v\|_{L^2(K)}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \| |\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n}|^{1/2} v\|_{L^2(\partial\Omega)}^2\tag{1.5.28}\\ &\geq \frac{1}{2} \enorm{v}^2.\tag{1.5.29} \end{align}
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 1.5.3. Continuity of \(a(\cdot,\cdot)\).

    For all \(w,v\in V_h\) we have that
    \begin{equation} a(w,v) \leq C_a \snorm{w} \enorm{v},\tag{1.5.30} \end{equation}
    where \(C_a = 5 + \linf{\sigma}/\mu\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    We bound every term of(1.4.5) in the form of(1.5.30). Beginning with the convection term, we apply Green’s formula
    \begin{equation} \int_\Omega (\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla w)\,v\,\mathrm{d}x = \int_{\partial\Omega} (\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,wv\,\mathrm{d}S - \int_\Omega (\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla v)\,w\,\mathrm{d}x.\tag{1.5.31} \end{equation}
    We bound the first term in(1.5.31) as
    \begin{align} \left| \int_{\partial\Omega} (\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,wv\,\mathrm{d}S \right| &\leq \int_{\partial\Omega} |\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,|w||v|\, \mathrm{d}S\\tag{1.5.32}\\ &\leq \left|\int_{\partial\Omega} |\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}| \,w^2\,\mathrm{d}S\right|^{1/2} \left|\int_{\partial\Omega} |\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}| \,v^2\,\mathrm{d}S\right|^{1/2}\tag{1.5.33}\\ &\leq \enorm{w} \enorm{v}\tag{1.5.34}\\ &\leq \snorm{w} \enorm{v}.\tag{1.5.35} \end{align}
    The second term in(1.5.31) is bounded as
    \begin{align} \left| \int_\Omega (\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla v)\,w\,\mathrm{d}x \right| &\leq \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \left|\int_K (\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla v)\,w\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\tag{1.5.36}\\ &\leq \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla v} \ltwok{w}\tag{1.5.37}\\ &\leq \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K^{1/2}\ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla v} \delta_K^{-1/2}\ltwok{w}\tag{1.5.38}\\ &\leq \left(\sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla v}^2 \right)^{1/2} \left(\sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K^{-1} \ltwok{w}^2\right)^{1/2}\tag{1.5.39}\\ &\leq \enorm{v}\snorm{w}.\tag{1.5.40} \end{align}
    Thus, the convection term is bounded as
    \begin{equation} \left|\int_\Omega (\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla w)\,v\,\mathrm{d}x\right| \leq 2\snorm{w}\enorm{v}.\tag{1.5.41} \end{equation}
    The reaction term is bounded by
    \begin{align} \label{eqn:a-cont-2} \left| \int_{\Omega} \sigma v w \, dx \right| &\leq \linf{\sigma} \|w\|_{L^2} \|v\|_{L^2}\tag{1.5.42}\\ &\leq \frac{\linf{\sigma}}{\mu}\snorm{w}\enorm{v}.\tag{1.5.43} \end{align}
    The inflow boundary term is bounded by
    \begin{align} \left| \int_{\Gamma_-} (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \, w v \, \mathrm{d}S \right| &\leq \int_{\Gamma_-} |\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{n}| \, |w| |v| \, \mathrm{d}S\tag{1.5.44}\\ &\leq \left( \int_{\Gamma_-} |\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,w^2\,\mathrm{d}S \right)^{1/2} \left( \int_{\Gamma_-} |\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,v^2\,\mathrm{d}S \right)^{1/2}\tag{1.5.45}\\ &\leq \left( \int_{\partial\Omega} |\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,w^2\,\mathrm{d}S \right)^{1/2} \left( \int_{\partial\Omega} |\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,v^2\,\mathrm{d}S \right)^{1/2}\tag{1.5.46}\\ &\leq \enorm{w} \enorm{v}\tag{1.5.47}\\ &\leq \snorm{w} \enorm{v}\tag{1.5.48} \end{align}
    For the SUPG term, we have
    \begin{align} \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla w &+ \sigma w)\, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x\tag{1.5.49}\\ &= \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla w)\, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x\tag{1.5.50}\\ & \qquad\qquad+ \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \, \sigma w\, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x.\tag{1.5.51} \end{align}
    We bound the first RHS term of(1.5.51) as
    \begin{align} &\left|\sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K\delta_K (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla w)\,(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x \right|\tag{1.5.52}\\ &\qquad\qquad\leq \left(\sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla w}^2 \right)^{1/2} \left(\sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot \nabla v}^2 \right)^{1/2}\tag{1.5.53}\\ &\qquad\qquad\leq \snorm{w}\enorm{v}\tag{1.5.54} \end{align}
    we bound the second term in(1.5.51) as
    \begin{align} \left|\sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K \, \sigma w\, (\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x\right| &\leq \linf{\sigma} \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \ltwok{w}\ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v}\tag{1.5.55}\\ &\leq \linf{\sigma} \left( \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \ltwok{w}^2 \right)^{1/2} \left( \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v}^2 \right)^{1/2}\tag{1.5.56}\\ &\leq \linf{\sigma} \delta_\text{max}^{1/2} \ltwo{w} \enorm{v}\tag{1.5.57}\\ &\leq \linf{\sigma} \delta_\text{max}^{1/2} \frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}} \snorm{w} \enorm{v}\tag{1.5.58}\\ &\leq \snorm{w} \enorm{v}\tag{1.5.59} \end{align}
    Adding together(1.5.41),(1.5.43),(1.5.48),(1.5.54) and(1.5.59), we have
    \begin{equation} a(w,v) \leq \left(5 + \frac{\linf{\sigma}}{\mu}\right) \snorm{w} \enorm{v}\tag{1.5.60} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 1.5.4. Boundedness of \(\mathcal{S}\).

    For all \(v\in H^1(\Omega)\) we have that
    \begin{equation} \ltwo{\mathcal{S}v} \leq \linf{\sigma_T}\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^2(\Omega\times\Omega)} \ltwo{v},\tag{1.5.61} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    By Cauchy-Schwarz, we have
    \begin{equation} \left| \int_{\Omega} \mathcal{K}(x,y) v(y)\, \mathrm{d}y \right|^2 \leq \left( \int_{\Omega} |\mathcal{K}(x,y)|^2 \, \mathrm{d}y \right) \|v\|_{L^2}^2.\tag{1.5.62} \end{equation}
    Thus, by definition of \(\mathcal{S}\)(1.4.2),
    \begin{align} \ltwo{\mathcal{S}v} &= \ltwo{\sigma_T(x)\int_\Omega \mathcal{K}(x,y) v(y)\mathrm{d}y}\\ &\leq \linf{\sigma_T} \left( \int_\Omega \left| \int_{\Omega} \mathcal{K}(x,y) v(y)\, \mathrm{d}y \right|^2 \mathrm{d}x \right)^{1/2}\tag{1.5.63}\\ &= \linf{\sigma_T} \left( \int_\Omega \int_\Omega \left| \mathcal{K}(x,y) \right|^2 \mathrm{d}x \mathrm{d}y \right)^{1/2} \,\ltwo{v}\tag{1.5.64}\\ &= \linf{\sigma_T}\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^2(\Omega\times\Omega)} \ltwo{v}.\tag{1.5.65} \end{align}
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 1.5.5. Continuity of \(s(\cdot,\cdot)\).

    For all \(w,v\in V_h\) we have that
    \begin{equation} s(w,v) \leq \tilde{C}_s \ltwo{w} \enorm{v},\tag{1.5.66} \end{equation}
    and thus (by relation of norms),
    \begin{equation} s(w,v) \leq C_s \enorm{w} \enorm{v}.\tag{1.5.67} \end{equation}
    where
    \begin{equation} C_s = \frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}}\tilde{C}_s = \linf{\sigma_T}\ldouble{\mathcal{K}}\frac{1}{\mu}\left(1 + \mu^{1/2}\delta_\text{max}^{1/2}\right)\tag{1.5.68} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    We have
    \begin{align} s(w,v) &= \int_{\Omega} (\mathcal{S}w)\, v \, \mathrm{d}x + \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \int_K \delta_K (\mathcal{S}w)\,(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla v)\, \mathrm{d}x \tag{1.5.69}\\ &\leq \ltwo{\mathcal{S}w}\ltwo{v} + \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \ltwok{\mathcal{S}w}\ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v}\tag{1.5.70}\\ &\leq \ltwo{\mathcal{S}w} \ltwo{v} + \delta_\text{max}^{1/2} \ltwo{\mathcal{S}w} \left( \sum_{K \in \mathscr{T}_h} \delta_K \ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v}^2 \right)^{1/2}\tag{1.5.71}\\ &\leq \ltwo{\mathcal{S}w} \ltwo{v} + \delta_\text{max}^{1/2} \ltwo{\mathcal{S}w} \enorm{v}\tag{1.5.72}\\ &\leq \ltwo{\mathcal{S}w} \left( \frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}} + \delta_\text{max}^{1/2} \right)\enorm{v}\\ \text{by Lemma \knowl{./knowl/xref/lem-bound-big-s.html}{\text{Lemma 1.5.4}}, }\tag{1.5.73}\\ &\leq \linf{\sigma_T}\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^2(\Omega\times\Omega)} \left( \frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}} + \delta_\text{max}^{1/2} \right)\ltwo{w}\enorm{v}\tag{1.5.74}\\ &\leq \linf{\sigma_T}\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^2(\Omega\times\Omega)} \left( \frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}} + \delta_\text{max}^{1/2} \right) \frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}} \enorm{w}\enorm{v}.\tag{1.5.75} \end{align}
    Using(1.4.4), we take
    \begin{align} \tilde{C}_s &= \linf{\sigma_T}\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^2(\Omega\times\Omega)} \left( \frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}} + \delta_\text{max}^{1/2} \right)\tag{1.5.76}\\ C_s &= \frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}}\tilde{C}_s\tag{1.5.77} \end{align}
    🔗
    🔗

    Corollary 1.5.6. Coercivity of \(a-s\).

    Suppose
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:a-s-coer-1} C_s \leq \frac{1}{2}.\tag{1.5.78} \end{equation}
    Then the bilinear form \(a-s\) is coercive. That is, there exists an \(\alpha>0\) such that for all \(v\in V_h\)
    \begin{equation} a(v,v) - s(v,v) \geq \alpha \enorm{v}^2.\tag{1.5.79} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    By Lemma 1.5.2 and Lemma 1.5.5, we have
    \begin{equation} a(v,v)-s(v,v) \geq \frac{1}{2}\enorm{v}^2 - C_s\enorm{v}\enorm{v}.\tag{1.5.80} \end{equation}
    By the assumption(1.5.78), we have
    \begin{equation} C_s \leq \frac{1}{2}.\tag{1.5.81} \end{equation}
    Therefore, taking \(\alpha = 1/2 - C_s\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation} a(v,v) - s(v,v) \geq \alpha \enorm{v}^2.\tag{1.5.82} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Corollary 1.5.7. Continuity of \(a-s\).

    For all \(w,v\in V_h\text{,}\) we have that
    \begin{equation} a(w,v) - s(w,v) \leq C_A \snorm{w}\enorm{v},\tag{1.5.83} \end{equation}
    where \(C_A = C_a + C_s\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    By Lemma 1.5.5 and Lemma 1.5.3, we have
    \begin{equation} a(w,v) - s(w,v) \leq C_a \snorm{w}\enorm{v} + C_s\enorm{w}\enorm{v} \leq (C_a + C_s)\snorm{w}\enorm{v}.\tag{1.5.84} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 1.5.8. Well-posedness.

    Let \(g\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\) with an extension \(\hat{g}\in H^1(\Omega)\) such that \(\hat{g}|_{\partial\Omega} = g\text{.}\) Then the variational problem of finding \(u\in H^1(\Omega)\) such that \(u = g\) on \(\Gamma_-\) and
    \begin{equation} a(u,v) - s(u,v) = l(v) \qquad \forall v\in H_0^1(\Omega)\tag{1.5.85} \end{equation}
    has a unique solution.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Define \(\tilde{u} = u - \hat{g}\in H_0^1(\Omega)\text{.}\) We seek
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:lax-1} a(\tilde{u},v) - s(\tilde{u},v) = \tilde{l}(v) \qquad \forall v\in H_0^1(\Omega)\tag{1.5.86} \end{equation}
    where
    \begin{equation} \tilde{l}(v) = l(v) - a(\hat{g},v) + s(\hat{g}, v).\tag{1.5.87} \end{equation}
    By Corollary 1.5.6 and Corollary 1.5.7, we have that \(a-s\) is coercive and bounded. Moreover, \(\tilde{l}\) is controlled by the SUPG-norm, with each term bounded since \(q\in L^2(\Omega)\) and \(\hat{g}\in H^1(\Omega)\text{.}\) Therefore, the Lax-Milgram theorem applies to give a unique \(\tilde{u}\) solving(1.5.86), hence a unique \(u = \tilde{u}+\hat{g}\) solving(1.5.85).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.5.4 Finite Element Error

    We consider the first term in the triangle inequality in(1.4.10). That is, we find an error estimate and convergence rate for the theoretical finite element solution \(u_h\in V_h\) of(1.4.8).
    🔗

    Theorem 1.5.9. C\’{e}a-type best-approximation estimate.

    Let \(u\in H^1_0\) be a solution of(1.2.1) with \(g=0\) and let \(u_h\in V_h\) be a solution of(1.4.8). Then we have the following best approximation result:
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u - u_h} \leq \left(1 + \frac{C_A}{\alpha}\right) \inf_{w_h \in V_h} \snorm{u - w_h}.\tag{1.5.88} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Let \(w_h\in V_h\) be arbitrary, we split the error as
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:best-appro-1} u - u_h = (u - w_h) + \underbrace{(w_h - u_h)}_{\phi_h \in V_h}.\tag{1.5.89} \end{equation}
    Applying coercivity to \(A = a-s\) (Corollary 1.5.6), we have
    \begin{equation} \alpha \enorm{\phi_h}^2 \leq A(\phi_h, \phi_h) = A(w_h-u_h, \phi_h).\tag{1.5.90} \end{equation}
    We insert the exact solution \(u\) into the first slot,
    \begin{align} A(w_h-u_h, \phi_h) &= A(w_h-u + u-u_h, \phi_h)\tag{1.5.91}\\ &= A(w_h-u, \phi_h) + A(u-u_h, \phi_h)\tag{1.5.92}\\ &= A(w_h-u, \phi_h),\tag{1.5.93} \end{align}
    where the last equality is due to Galerkin orthogonality. By continuity (Corollary 1.5.7), we have
    \begin{equation} \alpha \enorm{\phi_h}^2 \leq A(w_h-u, \phi_h) \leq C_A\snorm{w_h - u} \enorm{\phi_h}.\tag{1.5.94} \end{equation}
    Thus,
    \begin{equation} \enorm{\phi_h} \leq \frac{C_A}{\alpha}\snorm{w_h - u}.\tag{1.5.95} \end{equation}
    Applying the triangle inequality to(1.5.89),
    \begin{align} \enorm{u-u_h} &\leq \enorm{u - w_h} + \enorm{\phi_h}\tag{1.5.96}\\ &\leq \snorm{u - w_h} + \frac{C_A}{\alpha}\snorm{w_h - u}\tag{1.5.97}\\ &= \left( 1 + \frac{C_A}{\alpha} \right)\snorm{w_h - u}.\tag{1.5.98} \end{align}
    Taking the infimum over \(w_h\in V_h\text{,}\) we have the result
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u - u_h} \leq \left(1 + \frac{C_A}{\alpha}\right) \inf_{w_h \in V_h} \snorm{u - w_h}.\tag{1.5.99} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Corollary 1.5.10. Rates of Convergence.

    Let \(k\geq 1\) denote the piecewise polynomial degree of elements of \(V_h\text{.}\) Suppose the exact solution \(u\in H_0^r(\Omega)\) of(1.2.1) is such that \(r\) is sufficiently large for \(u\) to belong to the domain of the Lagrange interpolation operator. Then, there exists a constant \(C\) such that
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u-u_h} \leq C h^{\min\{k+1,r\} - 1/2} |u|_{H^r(\Omega)}.\tag{1.5.100} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    The proof follows that of~\cite{ashby2025nodally}, Corollary 3.5; we provide a brief overview. Denote by \(\mathcal{I}u\in V_h\) the piecewise polynomial Lagrange interpolant of \(u\) onto the finite element space \(V_h\text{.}\) Taking \(v_h = \mathcal{I}u\) in Theorem 1.5.9, we have
    \begin{align} \enorm{u-u_h}^2 &\leq \snorm{u-\mathcal{I}u}^2 \tag{1.5.101}\\ &= \mu \ltwo{u-\mathcal{I}u}^2 + \sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}} \delta_K \ltwok{\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla (u-\mathcal{I}u)}^2\tag{1.5.102}\\ &\qquad \qquad + \||\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}|^{1/2}(u-\mathcal{I}u)\|_{L^2(\partial\Omega)}^2 + \sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}} \delta_K^{-1}\ltwok{u-\mathcal{I}u}^2.\tag{1.5.103} \end{align}
    Each of these terms are bounded using standard interpolation estimates~\cite{ern2021finite}, which apply to norms over \(L^2(\Omega)\) and \(L^2(K)\text{.}\) The third term is first bounded by a trace estimate (see e.g.~\cite{evans2022partial}, Section 5.5) in terms of these norms. The following estimates combine to give(1.5.103):
    \begin{equation} \|u - \mathcal{I}u\|_{L^2(\Omega)} \leq C_I \, h_K^r \, |u|_{H^r(\Omega)},\tag{1.5.104} \end{equation}
    \begin{equation} \left\| \delta_K^{1/2} \, \mathbf{b} \cdot \nabla (u - \mathcal{I}u) \right\|_{L^2(K)} \leq C_I \, \|\mathbf{b}\|_{\infty,K} \, C_\delta^{1/2} \, h_K^{r - \tfrac{1}{2}} \, |u|_{H^r(\Omega)}, \tag{1.5.105} \end{equation}
    \begin{align} \left\||\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{n}|(u-\mathcal{I}u)\right\|_{L^2(\partial\Omega\cap K)} &\leq C_{\mathrm{tr}} \left( h_K^{-1/2} \|u - Iu\|_{L^2(K)} + h_K^{1/2} \|\nabla (u - Iu)\|_{L^2(K)} \right)\tag{1.5.106}\\ &\leq 2 C_{\mathrm{tr}} C_I \, h_K^{r - \tfrac{1}{2}} \, |u|_{H^r(\Omega)},\tag{1.5.107} \end{align}
    \begin{equation} \delta_K^{-1/2} \, \|u - \mathcal{I}u\|_{L^2(K)} \leq C_I \, C_\delta^{-1/2} \, h_K^{r - \tfrac{1}{2}} \, |u|_{H^r(\Omega)}.\tag{1.5.108} \end{equation}
    where \(C_{\mathrm{tr}}\) and \(C_\mathcal{I}\) are constants for trace and interpolation estimates, respectively.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.5.5 Fixed Point Error

    Now, we turn to the second error term in the triangle inequality(1.4.10). That is, we now derive a convergence rate for \(\enorm{u_h - u_h^{(n)}}\text{.}\) In doing so, we also find a rate in the \(L^2(\Omega)\)-norm.
    🔗

    Lemma 1.5.11. Contraction in mixed norms.

    Let \(u_h\in V_h\) be the solution of the stationary finite element problem(1.4.8). Take \(u_h^{(0)}\in V_h\) and denote by \(\{u_h^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_0}\) the sequence of solutions to the iterative problem(1.4.9). We have that, for all \(n\in \mathbb{N}_0\)
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u_h-u_h^{(n)}} \leq 2\tilde{C}_s \ltwo{u_h - u_h^{(n-1)}}.\tag{1.5.109} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Let \(e = u_h - u_h^{(n)}\) By coercivity of \(a(\cdot,\cdot)\) (Lemma 1.5.2),
    \begin{align} \frac{1}{2}\enorm{e}^2 &\leq a(e,e)\tag{1.5.110}\\ &= a(u_h,e) - a(u_h^{(n)},e)\tag{1.5.111}\\ &= s(u_h,e) + l(e) - s(u_h^{(n-1)},e) - l(e)\tag{1.5.112}\\ &= s(u_h-u_h^{(n-1)},e)\tag{1.5.113}\\ \text{by \knowl{./knowl/xref/lem-s-cont.html}{\text{Lemma 1.5.5}},}\tag{1.5.114}\\ &\leq \tilde{C}_s \ltwo{u_h-u_h^{(n-1)}}\enorm{e}.\tag{1.5.115} \end{align}
    Thus,
    \begin{equation} \enorm{e} \leq 2\tilde{C}_s \ltwo{u_h - u_h^{(n-1)}}.\tag{1.5.116} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 1.5.12. Convergence in \(L^2(\Omega)\)-norm.

    Let \(u_h\in V_h\) be the solution of the stationary finite element problem(1.4.8). Take \(u_h^{(0)}\in V_h\) and denote by \(\{u_h^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_0}\) the sequence of solutions to the iterative problem(1.4.9). We have that, for all \(n\in \mathbb{N}_0\)
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:conv-l2} \ltwo{u_h-u_h^{(n)}} \leq \left( \frac{2\tilde{C}_s}{\mu^{1/2}} \right)^n \ltwo{u_h - u_h^{(0)}}.\tag{1.5.117} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    By the relation of norms(1.5.2), we have
    \begin{equation} \ltwo{u_h-u_h^{(n)}} \leq \frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}}\enorm{u_h-u_h^{(n)} }.\tag{1.5.118} \end{equation}
    Applying Lemma 1.5.11 gives
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:cont-l2} \ltwo{u_h-u_h^{(n)}} \leq \frac{2\tilde{C}_s}{\mu^{1/2}} \ltwo{u_h - u_h^{(n-1)}}.\tag{1.5.119} \end{equation}
    Iterating(1.5.119) yields the desired convergence result.
    🔗
    🔗

    Corollary 1.5.13. Convergence in SUPG-norm.

    Let \(u_h\in V_h\) be the solution of the stationary finite element problem(1.4.8). Take \(u_h^{(0)}\in V_h\) and denote by \(\{u_h^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_0}\) the sequence of solutions to the iterative problem(1.4.9). We have that, for all \(n\in \mathbb{N}_0\)
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u_h-u_h^{(n)}} \leq 2\tilde{C}_s \left( \frac{2\tilde{C}_s}{\mu^{1/2}} \right)^{n-1} \ltwo{u_h - u_h^{(0)}}.\tag{1.5.120} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    The result follows from combining Lemma 1.5.11 and Theorem 1.5.12.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.5.6 Combined Error Estimate

    We can now populate the triangle inequality for iterative solution error originally proposed in(1.4.10).
    🔗

    Theorem 1.5.14. Error Estimate in the Deterministic Setting.

    Let \(u\) be the solution of(1.2.1) and suppose \(u_h\in V_h\) is the solution of the stationary finite element problem(1.4.8). Take \(u_h^{(0)}\in V_h\) and denote by \(\{u_h^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_0}\) the sequence of solutions to the iterative problem(1.4.9). We have that, for all \(n\in \mathbb{N}_0\)
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u-u_h^{(n)}} \leq C h^{\min\{k+1,r\} - 1/2} |u|_{H^r(\Omega)} + 2\tilde{C}_s \left( \frac{2\tilde{C}_s}{\mu^{1/2}} \right)^{n-1} \ltwo{u_h - u_h^{(0)}}.\tag{1.5.121} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    By the triangle inequality,
    \begin{equation} \enorm{u-u_h^{(n)}} \leq \enorm{u-u_h} + \enorm{u_h-u_h^{(n)}},\tag{1.5.122} \end{equation}
    and the result follows from applying Corollary 1.5.13 and Corollary 1.5.10.

    Subsection 1.5.7 Discussion

    The analysis above identifies two key constraints on the parameters of the problem.
    🔗
    First, the combined coercivity result for \(a - s\) (Corollary 1.5.6) and the contraction rate for source iteration (Theorem 1.5.12 and Corollary 1.5.13) both rely on the single constraint
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:Cs-condition} C_s = \frac{\linf{\sigma_T}\,\ldouble{\mathcal{K}}}{\mu} \left(1 + (\mu\,\delta_{\max})^{1/2}\right) \;\leq\; \tfrac{1}{2}.\tag{1.5.123} \end{equation}
    This is a subcriticality condition on the scattering operator: it requires the scattering cross section \(\sigma_T\text{,}\) kernel mass \(\ldouble{\mathcal{K}}\text{,}\) and stabilisation parameter \(\delta_{\max}\) to be small relative to the positivity constant \(\mu\text{.}\) Physically, this refers to absorption effects sufficiently dominating over scattering.
    🔗
    Second, the coercivity proof for \(a\) (Lemma 1.5.2) requires
    \begin{equation} \label{eqn:delta-condition} \delta_{\max} \;\leq\; \frac{\mu}{\linf{\sigma}^2},\tag{1.5.124} \end{equation}
    which prevents the indefinite SUPG cross term \(\delta_K \sigma v\,(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v)\) from overwhelming the streamline diffusion contribution. The standard SUPG scaling \(\delta_K = h_K\) used in our numerical experiments satisfies(1.5.124) for sufficiently fine meshes, namely \(h \leq \mu/\linf{\sigma}^2\text{.}\)
